Fashion & Home Product Tagging

Can a model understand what a product is by looking at where it appears?

Machine Learning
UC San Diego · CSE 158
ClassificationRandom ForestXGBoostFeature EngineeringLatent Factors

The problem

Product categorization is harder when the meaning of an item depends on its scene. Using Pinterest Shop the Look–derived fashion and home decor datasets, our team explored whether product geometry and scene-level context could improve automated category tagging.

The approach

01
Feature engineering

Derived bounding-box area, diagonal, center position, relative size, scene density, diversity, and other context-aware features.

02
Baseline models

Compared simple classifiers, including logistic regression and Naive Bayes, before building tree ensembles.

03
Advanced modeling

Evaluated Random Forest and XGBoost with engineered and latent factor features. Applied stratified splitting and tuned parameters with GridSearchCV.

A result worth showing

61%Best reported fashion accuracy
0.60Fashion F1 score
RF + latentTop fashion model

In the fashion dataset, adding latent factor features to a Random Forest achieved the best reported accuracy and F1 score. Results also highlighted class imbalance and the difficulty of generalizing across diverse product categories.

Why it matters

Tagging is a foundational layer of search, discovery, and recommendations. Better contextual features can help systems organize visual products more meaningfully, while thoughtful evaluation prevents headline accuracy from masking weaker performance for minority classes.
